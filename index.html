Only one of Nvidia's two money piles needs Jensen Huang.

Everyone is asking whether Huang keeps the job. Nvidia's own filing points to a better question. Its AI financing comes in two kinds. One kind is signed and binds the company no matter who runs it. The other kind is memoranda of understanding and options that Nvidia exercises "in our sole discretion." The second pile is about 3.5 times the first, and it is the part that depends on who sits in the CEO chair.

Signed · binds the company $170B Lease guarantees, AI-cloud capacity commitments and equity commitments in Note 10 of the 10-Q. A new CEO inherits all of it.
Not signed · at Nvidia's option $590B+ $500B+ of non-binding financing MOUs, plus an option on about 3.8 GW more of OpenAI's Ohio campus. Someone has to choose to sign each one.

The hypothesis

Nvidia's signed deals are safe. The unsigned ones shrink whether or not Huang stays, because a board that is investigating him will approve fewer open-ended guarantees.

This can be checked on a date. Nvidia files its next 10-Q after its Nov 17, 2026 results, the first filing written under board investigation. Note 10 shows whether the unsigned pile turned into signed obligations or stalled.

Bull case: the pile is converting

Guarantees rise above $108.5B, an MOU becomes a definitive agreement, or the 3.8 GW option is exercised. The governance scare had no effect on deal-making.

Helps: $SFTBY $CRWV $NBIS $IREN. Hypothesis wrong.

Bear case: the pile is frozen

Guarantees stay at $108.5B, the "may not lead to definitive agreements" line is unchanged with no first project named, or new board approval language appears for investments.

Hurts: $SFTBY $CRWV $NBIS $IREN. Mostly safe: $INTC $COHR $NOK $SNPS, TSMC. Hypothesis right.

The unsigned pile has already shrunk once, under Huang

This is the evidence that the discretionary layer moves. Between late July and mid-August 2026 the OpenAI guarantee under discussion went from about $250B to a signed $105B. Huang was fully in charge, and the cut still happened after Nvidia's credit default swaps hit a record.

Sep 2025

Letter of intent: Nvidia to invest "up to $100B" in OpenAI. In December the CFO said "we still haven't completed a definitive agreement."

Jul 28, 2026

Reports of talks to guarantee up to $250B of OpenAI's Ohio lease and finance $350B of chip purchases. Nvidia 5-year CDS hit a record 82 bp, and the stock fell about 5%.

Aug 10, 2026

Non-binding MOUs with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to "mobilize more than $500B" of third-party capital.

Aug 14, 2026

WSJ reports the OpenAI guarantee was cut to under $120B and limited to the first part of the campus.

Aug 17, 2026

Signed: guarantees capped at $105B for 4.25 GW at SB Energy's PORTS campus in Pike County, Ohio, with 20-year leases to OpenAI, plus $1.5B invested in SB Energy. The remaining ~3.8 GW stays an option.

Nov 8, 2026

Huang admits an affair in a message to staff. Independent directors open an investigation with outside counsel. The board says it has "no reason to believe" national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised.

Nov 11, 2026

WSJ reports the board is meeting to decide whether to replace him. Nvidia has named no successor. Fortune's read is that the board may keep him with less authority.

Nov 17, 2026

Q3 FY27 results after the close, followed by the 10-Q. This is the first filing written under board investigation. Note 10 will show whether the unsigned pile moved.

The market has priced Huang's risk into NVDA and nowhere else

Close of Fri Nov 6 (the last session before the news) to close of Wed Nov 11. Nvidia fell. The companies that depend on Nvidia's discretionary support rose with the rest of AI. Some of that is their own news: CoreWeave reported earnings this week. Still, nothing in these prices discounts the unsigned pile.

What to read in the Nov 17 10-Q

  1. Note 10 guarantees table, now $108.5B. A larger number means the unsigned pile is turning into signed obligations. The same number means it is on hold.
  2. The MOU sentence, "may not lead to definitive agreements." If it still reads that way three months later with no first project named, the $500B is still unsigned.
  3. The 3.8 GW option. Is it exercised, still open, or gone?
  4. Equity investment commitments, now $25B "subject to certain contingencies." These shrink if deals get repriced.
  5. New governance language: any board committee sign-off on investments or guarantees. A board can shrink the unsigned pile without firing anyone.

The strongest case against this

The board says it has confidence in Huang, so he may well stay. The MOUs were built so outside firms underwrite the capital, which keeps Nvidia's own role optional by design. The $500B Blackwell and Rubin backlog doesn't depend on any of this. And any successor would still want to sell GPUs.

Why the point survives: none of that brings the unsigned pile to zero risk. It only lowers it. A CEO who stays "with less" authority, or a successor who must answer to a board that is investigating him, signs fewer open-ended guarantees. The August cut from $250B to $105B shows how far that pile can move in three weeks.
